MEASURE THE CAVITY FLOOR TO CEILING BEFORE YOU SET THE SHELVES. Three tiers at 297 mm needs 891 mm of cavity inside an 851 mm cabinet, and the 320 L plate rating gives 600 mm exactly. If three shelves are fitted in 600 mm the pitch is 200 mm and a 270 mm bottle will not stand: pull one shelf from F1A and F1B, keep the third in F1C only, where 180 mm tubs clear it.
Either answer, the map below runs unchanged. Every backup sits in the depth of a tier, not on a third shelf.

Opening

  1. Squeeze the day's citrus. Fill rail bottles 1, 2 and 4. Bin yesterday's.
  2. F1A top: front row live, back row full. Refill from the F1A bottom tier.
  3. Check the F1B top tier. Anything low or past its day dot gets batched now, not mid-service.
  4. Check the F1C top tier. Refill empty squeeze bottles from the F1C bottom tier.
  5. Fresh delivery into F2A. Citrus below, everything else above.
  6. Fill the ice bin at station 2.
  7. Backup rows should be full at open and must not move during service. An empty backup row at open is last night's closing miss.

Mid-service

  1. Rail bottle empty: take the backup from the row behind it in F1A top.
  2. Squeeze bottle empty: take the double in F1C top, or refill from F1C bottom.
  3. Anything past its day dot comes out immediately.
  4. Refill the ice bin at the midpoint.
NOBODY OPENS THE DRY STORE DURING SERVICE. Dry is backup only. If the fridge is empty of something, the drink comes off the board until close.

Closing

  1. Day-dot everything opened today.
  2. Move tomorrow's ginger beer and soda from DRY into the F1B bottom tier to chill overnight.
  3. Bin the day's citrus and cut garnish.
  4. Rebuild the backup rows in F1A top and F1C top from tonight's prep.
  5. Wipe F1C. Purees do not sit dirty overnight.
